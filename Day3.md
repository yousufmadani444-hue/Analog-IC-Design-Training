## Design of the BGR Circuit

Bandgap Reference (BGR) – Temperature-Independent Voltage Generation

A Bandgap Reference (BGR) circuit is used to generate a stable and temperature-independent reference voltage, which is widely used in analog and mixed-signal circuits such as ADCs, DACs, voltage regulators, and biasing networks.

The operation of a BGR is based on the cancellation of two voltages with opposite temperature dependencies: a CTAT voltage and a PTAT voltage.

CTAT Voltage (VBE):
The base–emitter voltage (VBE) of a BJT exhibits a negative temperature coefficient. As temperature increases, VBE decreases at an approximate rate of −1.5 to −2 mV/°C. This voltage is referred to as Complementary To Absolute Temperature (CTAT).

PTAT Voltage (ΔVBE):
A Proportional To Absolute Temperature (PTAT) voltage is generated by operating two BJTs at different current densities, usually achieved by using different emitter areas. The difference in their base–emitter voltages is given by:

ΔVBE = VT · ln(N)

where VT = kT/q is the thermal voltage and N is the emitter area or current ratio. Since VT is directly proportional to temperature, ΔVBE increases linearly with temperature.

Temperature Cancellation:
The final reference voltage is obtained by summing the CTAT voltage and a scaled PTAT voltage:

VREF = VBE + K · ΔVBE

The scaling factor K is set using resistor ratios such that the negative temperature slope of VBE cancels the positive temperature slope of ΔVBE. When this condition is satisfied, the resulting reference voltage becomes largely independent of temperature.

Reference Voltage:
At room temperature, the generated reference voltage is approximately 1.2 V, which corresponds to the silicon bandgap voltage extrapolated to 0 K. This gives the bandgap reference its name.

Practical Considerations:
In practical BGR implementations, additional circuits such as startup circuits, amplifiers, and curvature compensation techniques are used to ensure correct operation, improve accuracy, and minimize higher-order temperature effects.
